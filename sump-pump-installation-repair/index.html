What Causes This in Pflugerville Homes

Central Texas storms can produce intense rainfall in a short period. Homes with a crawlspace or low-lying foundation area in Pflugerville rely on a working sump pump to keep that water from accumulating, particularly during the spring and early summer storm season.

Sump pumps are uncommon on slab homes, but a lot that drains toward the house, including some near Lake Pflugerville, can still collect water. We check whether a pump is the right fix at all.

Warning Signs

Signs You Need Sump Pump

Pump Running Constantly

A pump that never seems to shut off may have a stuck float switch or be undersized for the actual water volume.

Pump Not Turning On During Wet Conditions

A pump that stays silent during heavy rain, when it should be actively pumping, often has a failed float switch or motor.

Unusual Noise From the Pump

A grinding or rattling sound often means debris has entered the pump housing or the impeller is failing.

Visible Water Accumulation Despite Having a Pump

Standing water in a space with an installed pump means the system isn't keeping pace, whether from failure or undersizing.

Our Process

How We Diagnose and Fix It

For a failed pump, we test the float switch, check valve, and motor individually to identify which component failed, since a stuck float switch is a much smaller repair than a burned-out motor.

For a new installation, we size the pump to the space's actual water intake potential and install a check valve to prevent water from flowing back into the pit after each pump cycle. A battery backup option is also available for households concerned about power outages during storms.

Your Options

Repair vs. Replacement

Repair or replace depends on which component failed and the pump's age.

Repair

  • Fixes a stuck float switch or check valve.
  • Lower cost for an isolated component failure.
  • Makes sense on a newer pump.
  • Usually completed same visit.

Replace

  • Needed when the motor itself has failed.
  • Better fit for an aging or undersized pump.
  • Opportunity to add battery backup protection.
  • More reliable going into storm season.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I need a sump pump if I don't have a basement?

Homes with a crawlspace or a low-lying foundation area can still benefit from one, depending on the property's drainage and grading.

How often should a sump pump be tested?

Testing before storm season, by pouring water into the pit and confirming the pump activates, is a reasonable practice.

Is battery backup worth adding?

For households concerned about pump failure during a power outage in a storm, yes, since that's exactly when the pump is needed most.

Do you respond to sump pump failures at night?

Yes, our emergency line is answered 24/7, which matters most during active storm conditions.

How often should a sump pump be replaced?

Most pumps last 7 to 10 years with normal use; a unit that's already had more than one repair is often close to that point.

Can a sump pump be installed where there wasn't one before?

Yes, provided there's a suitable low point for a pit; we assess drainage and grading during the initial visit.
