Garbage Disposal Care: What You Should Never Put Down the Drain

By the Pflugerville Pro Plumbing team. Updated 2026.

A garbage disposal is more capable than people often assume. It's not built for everything, and the items that cause the most jams and premature failures are also some of the most commonly used in an ordinary kitchen.

Fibrous Vegetable Matter

Celery, corn husks, onion skins, and similar fibrous scraps can wrap around the impeller instead of breaking apart cleanly, which is one of the most common causes of a jammed disposal. The stringy fibers tangle rather than chop, and over time that repeated strain wears on the motor.

Grease, Oil, and Fat

These go down as a liquid but solidify as they cool, coating the inside of the disposal and the pipe just beyond it. That buildup is a leading cause of slow kitchen drains and recurring clogs, and disposals don't change this chemistry, grease still solidifies whether or not it passed through the blades first.

Bones, Pits, and Hard Shells

Small bones, fruit pits, and shellfish shells are hard enough to damage the impeller or jam the mechanism outright, and they're one of the more common reasons for an emergency disposal call after a holiday meal.

Starchy Foods and Coffee Grounds

Pasta, rice, and potato peels expand when they absorb water, and coffee grounds compact into a dense sludge, both of which contribute to blockages further down the drain line even when the disposal itself handles them without jamming.

Fruit Pits and Eggshells: A Mixed Case

Fruit pits are hard enough to damage the impeller and should always be avoided. Eggshells are debated, some people say the membrane can wrap around the impeller similar to fibrous vegetables, though a small amount rarely causes serious problems; if you're unsure, it's simplest to just compost or trash them instead.

Signs Your Disposal Habits Are Catching Up With You

A kitchen sink that drains slower than it used to is one early sign. Occasional gurgling from the disposal side. A mild odor even after running it, are others. All of these point to buildup accumulating in the line beyond the disposal itself, well before an actual clog forms.

Good Habits That Actually Help

Running cold water, not hot, while the disposal operates keeps grease and oils solid enough to be chopped and flushed through rather than melting and coating the pipe. Running the disposal regularly, even with just water, helps keep the mechanism from seizing due to disuse, which is a real issue in disposals that go unused for long stretches.

How Much a Disposal Can Actually Handle

A properly sized disposal handles typical daily food scraps well, small amounts of soft vegetable trimmings, rinsed plate scraps, and similar everyday waste. The problems arise less from any single use and more from a pattern of routinely putting the wrong things down it, grease, fibrous scraps, or large volumes of starchy food, over months and years.

When a Disposal Problem Is Actually a Plumbing Problem

If the disposal itself runs fine, spins freely, makes normal noise, but the sink still drains slowly or backs up, the issue has likely moved past the disposal into the drain line itself. At that point, disposal-specific troubleshooting won't resolve it, and it's worth having the line itself inspected instead.

Choosing a Replacement Unit

When a disposal does need replacing, horsepower is the main spec that matters for most households, a larger family with heavier daily use benefits from a higher-horsepower unit than a single person or couple would need. Noise level and included warranty are worth comparing too, since disposal quality varies meaningfully across price points.

Keep These Out of the Disposal

  • Fibrous scraps like celery, corn husks, and onion skins.
  • Grease, cooking oil, and fat drippings.
  • Bones, fruit pits, and shellfish shells.
  • Large amounts of pasta, rice, or potato peels.
  • Coffee grounds in significant quantity.

Common Questions

Is it okay to run the disposal with just water and no food?

Yes, running cold water while the disposal operates, and for a bit afterward, helps flush debris fully through the line.

What should I do if something falls in that shouldn't have?

Turn off and unplug the disposal, then manually remove the item; never reach in while it's powered on or plugged in.

Can ice cubes actually help clean a disposal?

Running ice through occasionally can help dislodge minor residue, though it's a minor maintenance habit, not a substitute for avoiding the items that actually cause jams.

Why does my disposal smell bad even though it seems to work fine?

Trapped food residue in the splash guard or lower housing is a common cause; a mix of ice and coarse salt run through occasionally can help scrub that residue loose.

Is it true that hot water helps flush grease through the disposal?

It's actually the opposite; hot water can melt grease into a liquid that later re-solidifies further down the line, while cold water keeps it firm enough to break apart and flush through cleanly.

How do I know if my disposal is properly sized for my household?

Frequent jamming or tripping despite reasonable use habits can be a sign of an undersized unit; we can assess and recommend an appropriately sized replacement if that's the issue.

Can a disposal problem cause a sink to smell even when it's not clogged?

Yes, residue trapped in the disposal chamber itself can cause odor without any actual blockage in the line, which is a different issue than a slow or clogged drain.

How long does a typical disposal replacement take?

Most replacements, swapping an existing unit for a new one at the same connection, are completed within an hour or two.

Do all disposals fit any kitchen sink?

Most standard sinks accommodate a standard disposal mount, but older or non-standard sink configurations sometimes need an adapter, which we check during installation.

Is it worth upgrading to a more powerful disposal during a kitchen remodel?

If you're already updating the kitchen, a higher-horsepower unit is a reasonable upgrade, especially for a household that cooks frequently and generates more food waste than average.

Can a disposal be too old to safely repair?

A unit that's well past 10 to 12 years old with a failed motor is usually more cost-effective to replace than repair, since the labor cost is similar either way and a new unit comes with a fresh warranty.

Does a disposal need a dedicated electrical circuit?

Most residential disposals run on a standard outlet or hardwired connection under the sink; a dedicated circuit isn't typically required for a standard household unit in most homes.

Should I turn off the disposal's power at the switch when not in use?

It's not necessary between uses, since the disposal only runs when actively switched on; the wall switch is simply the normal control for operating it.
