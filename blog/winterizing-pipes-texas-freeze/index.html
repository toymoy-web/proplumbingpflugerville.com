Preparing Your Pflugerville Home's Pipes for a Winter Freeze

By the Pflugerville Pro Plumbing team. Updated 2026.

Central Texas doesn't see winter the way northern climates do, which is exactly why a sudden hard freeze catches so many Pflugerville homes unprepared. Pipes here generally aren't insulated to the standard of a colder-climate home, and that gap is what causes the damage.

Why Texas Freezes Hit Different

In a climate where freezing temperatures are routine, pipes are typically run through conditioned interior space and insulated as standard practice. In Pflugerville, it's common for supply lines to run through an attic, exterior wall, or garage without the same level of protection, simply because a hard freeze is infrequent enough that it hasn't historically been treated as a given.

Before the Forecast Turns Cold

Disconnect and drain garden hoses, and shut off and drain any exterior faucets if yours have a separate shutoff. Locate any exposed pipe in a garage, crawlspace, or exterior wall and wrap it with foam pipe insulation, inexpensive and available at any hardware store. Know where your main water shutoff valve is, in case a pipe does fail despite precautions.

During an Active Freeze Warning

Let faucets fed by exposed or vulnerable pipe drip slightly, moving water is far less likely to freeze solid than standing water. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls to let warmer household air reach the pipes. Keep the thermostat consistent rather than lowering it overnight, even if you're not home.

If a Pipe Freezes or Bursts Anyway

A frozen but not yet burst pipe usually shows up as a fixture with no flow. Shut off the water at the affected fixture if possible. Never use an open flame to thaw a pipe, that's a real fire risk and can damage the pipe further. If a pipe has already burst, shut off the main water valve immediately and call.

Which Pipes Are Most at Risk in Pflugerville Homes

Exterior hose bibs, pipe running through an unheated garage, and any supply line in an attic without adequate insulation are the most common failure points we see during a hard freeze here. Pipes on exterior-facing walls with minimal insulation, common in older construction, are also more exposed than pipe run through fully interior walls.

A Note on Insurance Coverage

Sudden freeze damage, like a burst pipe from a cold snap, is often covered by homeowners insurance as sudden and accidental damage, though specific policy language varies. It's worth understanding your policy's terms before winter rather than during an active claim, and documenting any preventive steps you took can also support a claim if damage does occur despite precautions.

Preparing an Irrigation System for Freezing Weather

If your home has an irrigation system, it's worth draining and shutting it down ahead of the first hard freeze of the season. The buried lines and above-ground components, including the backflow prevention device, are vulnerable to the same freeze risk as household plumbing. A professional blowout, clearing the lines with compressed air, is the most thorough approach for a system with in-ground piping.

After the Freeze Passes

Once temperatures rise back above freezing, check every faucet in the house for normal flow, and look for any signs of dampness around pipe that was exposed during the cold snap. A pipe that survived the freeze without bursting can still have hairline damage that only shows up as a slow leak days later, so it's worth a careful check even if nothing seemed to go wrong at the time.

What Makes This Region's Freezes Especially Damaging

It's not just that Pflugerville pipes are less insulated than a northern climate's. It's also that Texas freezes tend to arrive suddenly and briefly, giving homeowners less lead time to prepare than a region with a predictable, gradual winter onset. A forecast that shifts from mild to a hard freeze within a day or two is common here, which is exactly why having your preparations already in place, rather than scrambling once a freeze warning is issued, matters so much.

Freeze-Prep Checklist

  • Disconnect and drain garden hoses.
  • Insulate exposed pipe in garages, attics, and exterior walls.
  • Know the location of your main water shutoff valve.
  • Let vulnerable faucets drip during a hard freeze.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls.
  • Review your homeowners insurance policy's freeze damage coverage.
  • Drain or blow out an irrigation system before the first hard freeze.

Common Questions

How cold does it actually need to get for pipes to freeze here?

Sustained temperatures below freezing for several hours put unprotected pipe at real risk, which is why even a short but hard Texas cold snap can cause damage that a similar temperature drop wouldn't cause in a climate built for it.

Is letting a faucet drip actually necessary, or just extra caution?

It genuinely helps; moving water resists freezing far better than standing water in a pipe, and it's one of the most effective low-cost precautions available.

Can insulating pipe after the fact still help mid-winter?

Yes, it's worth doing as soon as you identify exposed pipe, even partway through the season, rather than waiting until next year.

Should I disconnect my water heater during an extended freeze?

Not typically necessary for a standard cold snap; the water heater itself is usually in a protected interior space, though its exposed connections are worth checking.

How long does frozen pipe damage take to show up?

Sometimes immediately once it thaws and the crack becomes apparent; other times a day or two later as the ice fully melts and water finds its way out.

Do I need to winterize my irrigation system every year?

Yes, if your area sees any hard freezes, since the risk to buried and exposed irrigation components resets each winter regardless of what was done the year before.

What if I'm traveling during a forecasted freeze?

Leaving the thermostat at a consistent temperature, not turned down to save energy, and having a neighbor check on the house if a hard freeze is expected during an extended absence are both worth arranging ahead of time.

Why do Texas freezes seem to cause more damage than expected?

Homes here generally aren't built with the same pipe insulation standards as consistently cold climates, and freezes often arrive with little lead time, both of which add up to more risk per cold snap than the same temperature drop would cause elsewhere.

Should I cover outdoor faucets with insulated covers every winter?

Yes, an inexpensive foam faucet cover is a simple, reusable precaution worth applying every year ahead of the first expected freeze, not just during an unusually cold season.

Do pipes in a slab foundation face the same freeze risk as elevated pipes?

Generally less risk, since slab-set pipe benefits from the ground's insulating effect; it's exposed pipe in attics, garages, and exterior walls that faces the greater freeze risk.
