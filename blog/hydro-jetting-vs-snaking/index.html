Hydro Jetting vs. Snaking: Which Clears a Clogged Drain Better?

By the Pflugerville Pro Plumbing team. Updated 2026.

Not every clogged drain needs the same fix. Snaking and hydro jetting solve different versions of the same problem. Using the wrong one is a common reason a clog comes right back within weeks.

How Snaking Works

A drain snake is a flexible cable, manually fed through the pipe to physically break through or hook onto whatever's blocking it. It's fast, effective for a single, isolated obstruction like a wad of hair or a foreign object, and it's what most store-bought tools and quick service calls rely on.

How Hydro Jetting Works

Hydro jetting uses a specialized nozzle to spray high-pressure water in multiple directions as it's pulled back through the line, scouring the entire interior surface of the pipe rather than just punching a path through the clog itself. It clears grease coating, mineral scale, and root intrusion far more thoroughly than a snake can.

When Snaking Is the Right Call

A first-time clog, a clear single obstruction, or a fixture that's never had a recurring problem is usually a good candidate for snaking. It's faster and less involved, and for a genuinely isolated blockage, it resolves the issue completely.

When Hydro Jetting Is the Right Call

A drain that clogs again within weeks of being snaked, a kitchen line with ongoing grease buildup, or a sewer line showing root intrusion on a camera inspection are all better matched to jetting. It costs more than a basic snaking, but it lasts longer precisely because it addresses the coating on the pipe wall, not just the immediate blockage.

Why a Camera Inspection Usually Comes First

Before jetting, a camera inspection confirms two things: what's actually causing the problem, and whether the pipe itself is in good enough condition to handle jetting safely. Older or already-weakened pipe gets a lower pressure setting specifically to clean effectively without risking further damage, which is a judgment call best made after actually seeing the pipe's condition rather than guessing.

Cost and Time Differences

Snaking a single accessible clog is typically the faster, lower-cost option, often resolved within the same visit it's diagnosed. Hydro jetting takes longer because it treats the full length of the line rather than just the blockage point, and pricing reflects that additional time and specialized equipment, though it's a better value over time for a line with a recurring problem.

Commercial Kitchens Are a Different Case

For a restaurant or commercial kitchen, grease accumulation happens faster than in a residential setting, and periodic jetting on a set schedule, rather than waiting for a full backup, is often the more practical maintenance approach. Residential kitchens rarely need this kind of proactive schedule, but a household that fries food often or runs a large family through the kitchen daily may see more benefit from it than a typical single-person household.

What to Expect During a Jetting Service

We access the line through an existing cleanout wherever possible, minimizing disruption to your home. The equipment itself is loud but contained, and most residential jetting jobs are completed within an hour or two depending on the line's length and condition.

Signs You've Been Snaking the Same Problem Repeatedly

If you've called for drain service more than twice in a year for what seems like the same kitchen or bathroom line, that pattern itself is useful diagnostic information. It suggests the underlying buildup was never actually cleared, only pushed aside temporarily, which is exactly the situation hydro jetting is built to resolve permanently rather than repeating the same partial fix.

A Practical Way to Decide

If this is your first call for this particular drain, snaking is a reasonable, lower-cost starting point. If it's your second or third call for the same line within a relatively short span, it's worth skipping straight to a camera inspection and jetting rather than paying for another temporary fix.

Snaking vs. Hydro Jetting

Snaking

  • Fast and lower cost.
  • Good for a single isolated clog.
  • Doesn't clear buildup on the pipe wall.
  • Higher chance of recurrence for grease or scale.

Hydro Jetting

  • Clears the full interior pipe diameter.
  • Effective against grease, scale, and root intrusion.
  • Longer-lasting results.
  • Camera inspection recommended first.

Common Questions

Can hydro jetting damage my pipes?

At an appropriately matched pressure setting, no; older or already-compromised pipe is jetted at a lower pressure specifically to avoid that risk.

Is a camera inspection always needed before jetting?

It's strongly recommended, since it confirms the pipe can safely handle jetting and shows exactly what's causing the recurring problem.

Why did my snaked drain clog again so quickly?

Snaking clears a path through a clog but doesn't remove the coating on the pipe wall that caused it; if that coating is still there, it tends to rebuild a blockage fairly fast.

Is hydro jetting messy or disruptive to the household?

Not typically; the equipment works through existing cleanouts or access points, so there's minimal disruption to the rest of the house during the process.

Can I request hydro jetting even for a first-time clog?

You can, though for a genuinely isolated first-time blockage, snaking is often the more cost-effective starting point; we'll give you an honest read on which fits your situation.

How often should a residential line be jetted preventively?

Most homes don't need a preventive schedule at all; jetting is typically reserved for lines with a documented recurring problem rather than routine maintenance.

Does hydro jetting help with tree root intrusion in a sewer line?

Yes, for roots growing into the pipe interior; if the roots entered through a crack, though, the crack itself may still need a separate repair.

What's a reasonable number of repeat calls before switching to jetting?

If a snaking service hasn't held for more than a few weeks on two separate occasions, that's a reasonable point to move to a camera inspection and jetting instead.

Does jetting work on both drain lines and sewer lines?

Yes, the equipment and approach apply to both, though sewer line jetting typically follows a camera inspection given the larger scope and higher stakes of that line.

How much does the length of the affected line affect jetting cost?

Longer lines generally take more time to jet thoroughly, which is a real factor in pricing alongside the type of buildup found.

Can hydro jetting be used as a one-time deep clean even without a specific problem?

It's generally reserved for lines with a documented issue rather than routine maintenance, since jetting a line with no real buildup doesn't provide much additional benefit over its cost for most homeowners.

Does the season affect when jetting is most effective?

Not significantly for residential lines; commercial kitchens sometimes see heavier grease loads during high-volume seasons, but jetting effectiveness itself isn't meaningfully tied to time of year.
