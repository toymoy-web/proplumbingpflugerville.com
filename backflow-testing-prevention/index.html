Local Causes

Local Causes in Pflugerville

Irrigation systems are the most common reason a Pflugerville property has a backflow prevention device in the first place. A drop in municipal water pressure combined with a submerged sprinkler head can otherwise siphon lawn chemicals and standing water back into the home's supply line.

Recognizing the Problem

Signs Backflow Testing Can't Wait

Annual Testing Notice

Many Pflugerville properties with irrigation systems receive an annual reminder that their backflow device needs testing to stay compliant.

Water Discoloration or Odd Taste After Irrigation Use

An unusual taste or color in tap water shortly after irrigation runs can indicate a backflow device isn't sealing properly.

Visible Device Leaking or Discharging

A backflow preventer that continuously discharges water at its relief valve usually needs a repair or rebuild kit.

New Irrigation or Commercial Connection

Any new irrigation system or commercial water connection typically requires a backflow device to be installed as part of the connection.

The Fix

How the Fix Actually Works

Annual backflow testing involves connecting calibrated test equipment to the device's test cocks and confirming both check valves hold pressure correctly under simulated backflow conditions, with results reported per local requirements.

If a device fails testing, we rebuild or replace the internal components rather than assuming the whole assembly needs replacement, since most failures are a worn seal or spring rather than a failure of the device body itself.

Received a Testing Notice?

If you've gotten a notice that your backflow device is due for annual testing, we handle the test and any required documentation in the same visit.

Weighing Options

Choosing the Right Fix

Most backflow situations are a straightforward test-and-certify, though a failed device does need a decision on repair versus replacement.

Rebuild

  • Replaces worn seals, springs, or check valve components.
  • Lower cost than a full device replacement.
  • Common fix for a device that fails testing.
  • Usually completed same visit.

Full Replacement

  • Needed when the device body itself is damaged.
  • Makes sense for very old or discontinued models.
  • Ensures the newest compliance standards are met.
  • Better long-term reliability.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I really need annual backflow testing?

If your property has an irrigation system or certain commercial connections, yes, it's typically an annual requirement, not optional.

What happens if my device fails the test?

We can usually rebuild it the same visit; a full replacement is only needed if the device body itself is damaged.

Can I install a backflow device myself?

It's not something we'd recommend, since correct installation and testing typically require certified equipment and documentation.

Does every home need a backflow prevention device?

Not every home; it's typically required for properties with irrigation systems or certain other cross-connections to the water supply.

Can a backflow device freeze in winter?

An above-ground device in an exposed location can freeze during a rare hard freeze; insulating it or draining it ahead of a cold snap is worth doing if your device isn't in a heated enclosure.

How long does an annual test usually take?

Most residential tests are completed in well under an hour, start to finish.
