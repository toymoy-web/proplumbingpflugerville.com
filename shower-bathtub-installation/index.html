Local Causes

Local Causes in Pflugerville

Older Pflugerville homes with original 1980s and 1990s single-handle shower valves often have worn cartridges that cause temperature swings or won't fully shut off. Homeowners frequently discover this only once they've already started a bathroom remodel and removed the old fixture.

Tub-to-shower conversions are common in established homes around Highland Park and Settlers Ridge, where the original bathtub and valve have had decades of use.

Recognizing the Problem

Signs Shower & Tub Can't Wait

Valve Won't Fully Shut Off

A shower valve that keeps dripping after being turned off usually has a worn cartridge, the same failure mode as a leaking faucet.

Sudden Temperature Swings

Water that suddenly runs hot or cold when another fixture is used elsewhere in the house points to a valve that isn't properly balancing pressure.

Leaking Behind the Wall

Water damage on the wall or ceiling below a shower often means a drain or valve connection is leaking behind the tile rather than at a visible fixture.

Planning a Tub-to-Shower Conversion

Converting an underused tub to a walk-in shower requires reworking the drain location and waterproofing, not just swapping the fixture.

The Fix

How the Fix Actually Works

For a valve repair, we replace the cartridge or full valve body to match the temperature-balancing standard required by current code, which also resolves the scalding risk older, non-balancing valves can pose.

For a new installation or conversion, we verify the drain location, slope, and waterproofing membrane before setting any new fixture, since a shower pan that isn't properly sloped or sealed is a common source of hidden leaks discovered only after tile work is complete.

Weighing Options

Choosing the Right Fix

A repair keeps the existing fixture in place; a conversion or new install changes the space itself.

Valve Repair

  • Fixes temperature or shutoff issues.
  • Keeps existing tile and fixture.
  • Lower cost than a full renovation.
  • Brings older valves up to current balancing standards.

New Installation or Conversion

  • Full tub-to-shower or fixture replacement.
  • Addresses drain slope and waterproofing.
  • Opportunity to upgrade fixtures and finishes.
  • Larger scope, coordinated with any tile work.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my shower suddenly run cold when someone flushes a toilet?

That's a valve that isn't properly pressure-balancing; newer code-compliant valves are designed specifically to prevent this.

Do I need to replace tile to fix a leaking shower valve?

Not always. Many valve repairs can be done through the existing access panel or a small opening without disturbing surrounding tile.

What's involved in a tub-to-shower conversion?

It typically involves reworking the drain, adding a proper waterproofing membrane, and coordinating with tile or surround installation.

How do I know if a leak is coming from the shower valve or the drain?

A leak that appears during use points to the valve or shower head; one that appears with standing water points more toward the drain seal.

Can you match a new shower fixture to my existing bathroom finish?

Yes, we can source fixtures in a range of finishes to match or complement existing hardware.
