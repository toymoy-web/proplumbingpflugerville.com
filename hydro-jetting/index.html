Why It Happens

Why This Happens in Pflugerville

Grease buildup in kitchen lines and root intrusion in older sewer lines are the two most common reasons we recommend hydro jetting over standard snaking in Pflugerville. Both problems tend to recur quickly if the pipe wall itself isn't actually cleared.

When a snake keeps coming back on an Old Town or Highland Park drain, jetting scours the pipe wall that the snake leaves behind, which is why the clog stays gone longer.

Signs to Watch For

How to Tell You Need This

Clog Returns After Snaking

A drain that clogs again within a few weeks of being snaked usually has buildup coating the whole pipe wall, not just a single obstruction.

Multiple Slow Drains

Several slow-draining fixtures at once can indicate grease or scale buildup throughout the branch or main line.

Known Root Intrusion

A camera inspection showing roots growing into the line is a strong candidate for jetting before or instead of a repair.

Commercial Kitchen Grease Buildup

Restaurants and commercial kitchens often need periodic jetting to manage grease accumulation between trap servicing.

How It Works

Our Hydro Jetting Process

We run a camera inspection first to confirm the line can safely handle jetting. A severely compromised or very old pipe sometimes needs a different approach. Use a specialized nozzle that sprays high-pressure water in multiple directions to scour the entire interior surface as it's pulled back through the line.

Pressure is adjusted based on the pipe's material and condition; older galvanized or clay pipe gets a lower pressure setting than modern PVC to clean effectively without risking damage to already-weakened pipe walls.

Decision Guide

Which Option Fits Your Situation

Jetting isn't always the first step; the camera inspection determines whether it's the right tool for a given line.

Standard Snaking

  • Clears an isolated, one-time obstruction.
  • Lower cost for a simple blockage.
  • Doesn't remove buildup coating the pipe wall.
  • Higher chance of recurrence for grease or scale.

Hydro Jetting

  • Clears buildup along the entire pipe wall.
  • More effective against grease and root intrusion.
  • Longer-lasting results for recurring clogs.
  • Pressure adjusted to the pipe's actual condition.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Is hydro jetting safe for older pipes?

Yes, at an appropriately lowered pressure setting; the camera inspection beforehand tells us what the pipe can handle.

How is hydro jetting different from snaking?

Snaking punches a hole through a clog; jetting scours the entire interior pipe wall, which is why it lasts longer against grease and buildup.

Can hydro jetting remove tree roots?

Yes, for roots growing into the pipe interior, though if the roots have entered through a crack, the crack itself may still need repair.

Do commercial kitchens need jetting more often?

Yes, grease accumulation in commercial lines is typically faster than residential, so more frequent jetting is common.

Will jetting clear a line that's already fully blocked?

Usually yes, though a completely blocked line sometimes needs an initial mechanical clearing first to create an opening for the jetting nozzle to pass through.

Does jetting need to be repeated on a regular schedule?

For a residential line with a one-time buildup issue, no; for a commercial kitchen line, periodic jetting on a set schedule is common practice.
