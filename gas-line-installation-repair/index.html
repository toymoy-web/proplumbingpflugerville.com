The Pflugerville Factor

The Local Factor Behind This Problem

New gas line requests in Pflugerville are frequently tied to a kitchen renovation adding a gas range, an outdoor kitchen or fire feature, or a tankless water heater conversion. Tankless units generally require a larger gas supply line than the standard tank they're replacing.

Outdoor kitchens and fire features in newer Falcon Pointe and Blackhawk backyards are a common reason for a new gas line, and every connection is pressure-tested before we leave.

Do You Need This?

Do You Need Gas Line?

Smell of Gas Near a Line or Fitting

Any gas odor should be treated as urgent. Leave the area and call immediately rather than investigating yourself.

Appliance Not Getting Full Flame or Heat

A weak flame at a gas appliance can mean a undersized line or a partial blockage, not necessarily the appliance itself.

Hissing Sound Near a Gas Connection

A hissing sound at a fitting is a sign of an active leak and should be treated as an emergency.

New Appliance Requiring a Gas Connection

A new range, dryer, or outdoor fixture needing gas service is the most common reason for a new line installation.

Emergency Response

Gas Emergency: Safety Steps Before You Call Us

A gas leak is a safety emergency before it is a plumbing job. Follow these steps in this order.

Leave right away

Get everyone and every pet outside. Do not flip switches, use lighters or start a car in the garage.

Call from outside

Call 911 or your gas utility emergency line from a safe distance. Do not go back in to look for the source.

Wait for the all clear

Do not re-enter until the utility or fire department says it is safe. Gas collects in closed rooms.

Then call us for the repair

Once the line is made safe, we inspect the piping, repair or replace failed sections and leak test the line before service resumes.

Our Approach

How We Handle It

For a new line, we calculate the required pipe diameter based on total BTU demand across every gas appliance on that line. Undersizing a line to save on materials is a common cause of appliances not receiving adequate gas pressure.

Every gas line installation or repair is pressure-tested before we connect any appliance, checking for leaks at every joint and fitting rather than assuming a connection is sound because it was tightened correctly.

Your Choices

What Your Options Look Like

Gas line work is generally straightforward once the scope is defined, but sizing and code compliance are not areas to shortcut.

Repair Existing Line

  • Fixes a leak or damaged section.
  • Makes sense on an otherwise adequate line.
  • Faster than a new installation.
  • Pressure-tested before completion.

New Line Installation

  • Needed for new appliances or fixtures.
  • Sized to total BTU demand across the line.
  • Required for most tankless water heater conversions.
  • Includes code-compliant shutoff valve placement.

Answers

Questions We Hear Often

I smell gas, what should I do?

Leave the area immediately, don't operate any switches or flames, and call from a safe distance. This is treated as an emergency, not a scheduled repair.

Do I need a permit for a new gas line?

Yes, gas line work typically requires a permit and inspection under local code, which we handle as part of the installation.

Can you run a gas line for an outdoor kitchen or fire pit?

Yes, we size and run lines for outdoor grills, fire features, and other exterior gas fixtures.

Does switching to tankless always require a bigger gas line?

In most cases yes, since tankless units draw significantly more gas at peak demand than a standard tank. We check the existing line before quoting a conversion.

How long does gas line installation typically take?

A straightforward new line for a single appliance is often completed in a few hours, including the pressure test. Longer runs or multiple appliances take more time.

Should I call you or the gas company first for a gas smell?

Leave the home and call 911 or your gas utility first. Call us afterward for the pipe repair, inspection or new connection.

Can you hook up a gas stove or barbecue line?

Yes. We run and connect the line, install the shutoff and leak test the connection.

Do you work on gas fireplaces and inserts?

We handle the gas supply and connection to a fireplace or insert. If the unit itself needs repair, tell us what it is doing when you call.
