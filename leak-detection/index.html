Local Causes

Local Causes in Pflugerville

Two local conditions drive most of our leak detection calls. Homes built in the 1980s and 1990s with galvanized or polybutylene supply lines develop pinhole leaks as those materials corrode from the inside, often inside a wall or under a slab where nothing is visibly wrong until the water bill spikes. Newer homes sit on expansive clay soil that shifts with the season, and that movement stresses supply and drain lines running under the slab until a fitting or a length of pipe finally gives way.

A bill spike in Springbrook, Highland Park, or Falcon Pointe usually sends us to the same few places first: the water heater connection, the irrigation lines out front, and the slab itself.

Recognizing the Problem

Signs Leak Detection Can't Wait

Rising Water Bill With No New Usage

A jump in usage with nothing changed in the household is the most common early sign of a hidden leak.

Sound of Running Water

Water audible in the walls or floor with every fixture shut off usually means a supply line leak, not a fixture problem.

Warm Spot on the Floor

A warm patch on a slab floor is a strong sign of a hot water line leaking underneath it.

Mold or Musty Smell With No Visible Source

A persistent musty smell without a visible spill often means moisture accumulating behind a wall or under flooring.

The Fix

How the Fix Actually Works

We start with the water meter. Shutting off every fixture in the house and watching whether the meter still moves confirms there's an active leak before we spend time locating it. From there, acoustic listening equipment picks up the sound of water escaping under pressure, and it's often precise enough to narrow the search to a specific section of wall or floor.

For a leak we can't pinpoint acoustically, we use thermal imaging to spot the temperature difference a hot water leak leaves in a slab or wall, which lets us confirm the leak's location before opening anything up rather than cutting exploratory holes.

Not Sure It's a Leak Yet?

If your water bill jumped, you hear water running with everything off, or a spot on the floor feels warm for no reason, that's exactly what our leak detection equipment is built to confirm before any repair work starts.

Weighing Options

Choosing the Right Fix

Once a leak is located, the repair itself depends on where it sits and what the pipe is made of.

Guessing and Cutting Blind

  • Opens walls or slab without confirming the location first.
  • Often misses the actual leak point.
  • More demolition than necessary.
  • No way to confirm the leak is actually fixed.

Acoustic + Thermal Detection

  • Confirms the leak's location before any cutting.
  • Minimizes demolition to the actual leak point.
  • Distinguishes supply line leaks from drain line leaks.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you find a leak without cutting into my walls?

In most cases, yes. Acoustic and thermal equipment locates the leak first, so any cutting is limited to the confirmed spot.

How do I know if I have a slab leak specifically?

A warm spot on the floor, along with a rising water bill, is the strongest early sign; see our slab leak detection and repair page for more detail.

Does a leak in one spot mean the whole system needs replacing?

Not necessarily. A single pinhole leak in an otherwise sound system is often a targeted repair, not a full repipe.

Do you offer leak detection on weekends?

Yes, our line is answered 24/7 for both scheduled inspections and active leak emergencies.
