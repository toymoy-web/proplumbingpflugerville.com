Local Causes

Local Causes in Pflugerville

Galvanized pipe from 1980s and 1990s Pflugerville construction corrodes from the inside over decades until a section finally gives way, often without warning. On newer construction, ground movement from expansive clay soil puts stress on buried and slab-adjacent lines, and a sudden freeze during a rare Central Texas cold snap can also crack a pipe that was already weakened by corrosion or soil stress.

A burst line can happen anywhere in town, from an old galvanized run in Old Town to a failed fitting in a newer Blackhawk home. The first step is the same in both: shut the main and call.

Recognizing the Problem

Signs Burst Pipe Repair Can't Wait

Sudden Drop in Water Pressure

A sharp, sudden pressure drop across the house can mean a break somewhere in the main line.

Water Pooling With No Clear Source

Water appearing on a floor or ceiling with no obvious cause is a common sign of a burst pipe inside a wall or above a ceiling.

Unusually High Water Meter Reading

A meter that's still spinning with every fixture off confirms an active break somewhere in the system.

Sound of Rushing Water

A hissing or rushing sound inside a wall, especially right after a cold snap, often means a pipe has already cracked.

The Fix

How the Fix Actually Works

The first step on any burst pipe call is locating and shutting off the nearest isolation valve to stop the flow of water before it causes more damage. That's why knowing the location of your main shutoff valve matters before an emergency happens.

Once the water is off, we identify the failed section, cut it back to sound pipe on both sides, and either splice in a repair coupling for a localized break or recommend a broader repipe if the surrounding pipe shows the same corrosion pattern.

Weighing Options

Choosing the Right Fix

A burst pipe repair is usually urgent by nature, but the scope of the fix still depends on what caused the break.

Localized Spot Repair

  • Fixes a single isolated break.
  • Faster and lower cost.
  • Makes sense on otherwise sound pipe.
  • Gets water restored quickly.

Broader Repipe

  • Makes sense when surrounding pipe shows the same corrosion.
  • Prevents the next break from happening soon after.
  • Better long-term value on 1980s-90s galvanized systems.
  • See our whole-home repipe page.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is my main water shutoff valve?

Usually near the water meter at the street or where the main line enters the house; we can show you during any service call so you're prepared for next time.

Can a burst pipe happen from cold weather in Texas?

Yes, a hard freeze can crack pipe that's already weakened by corrosion or exposed to outside temperatures, even in a normally mild climate.

Do you respond to burst pipes at night?

Yes, our emergency line is answered 24/7 for active breaks and flooding situations.

Will one burst pipe mean the whole house needs repiping?

Not necessarily. We only recommend a broader repipe when the surrounding pipe shows the same corrosion pattern as the break itself.
