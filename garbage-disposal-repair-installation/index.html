Why It Happens

Why This Happens in Pflugerville

The most common disposal failure isn't age. It's what goes down it. Fibrous vegetable matter and grease that solidifies against the impeller are the leading causes of jams and leaks we see, regardless of the unit's brand or how new it is.

Disposals in busy family kitchens across Falcon Pointe, Blackhawk, and Springbrook jam most often from fibrous scraps and grease. A quick diagnosis tells us whether it needs a reset or a replacement.

Signs to Watch For

How to Tell You Need This

Humming But Not Spinning

A disposal that hums without turning usually has a jammed impeller, which is often clearable without replacing the unit.

Leaking From the Bottom

A leak at the base of the disposal typically means a worn seal, which is a repair rather than a full replacement in most cases.

Frequent Tripping of the Reset Button

Repeated overload trips can mean the disposal is undersized for your household's usage or the motor is wearing out.

Unusual Grinding Noise

A grinding or rattling sound often means something solid, like a utensil, has fallen into the chamber.

How It Works

Our Garbage Disposal Process

For a jammed unit, we manually free the impeller using the disposal's reset mechanism and a hex key through the bottom access point, which resolves the majority of jam calls without opening the unit itself.

For a leak, we identify whether it's coming from the sink flange gasket, the dishwasher connection, or the disposal body itself, since each has a different, specific fix rather than a single generic repair.

Decision Guide

Which Option Fits Your Situation

Repair usually makes sense unless the motor has actually failed or the unit is significantly undersized for the household.

Repair

  • Fixes a jam, seal leak, or reset issue.
  • Lower cost than replacement.
  • Keeps a unit that otherwise works fine.
  • Usually completed in one visit.

Replace

  • Makes sense if the motor has failed.
  • Better fit if the unit is undersized for usage.
  • Newer units run quieter and more efficiently.
  • Resolves chronic tripping issues.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Why won't my disposal reset button stay in?

A reset button that won't stay engaged usually means the motor is drawing too much current, often from a persistent jam or a failing motor.

What should never go down a disposal?

Fibrous vegetable matter, grease, and large bones are the most common causes of jams and premature wear, regardless of the unit's rating.

Can you install a disposal where there wasn't one before?

Yes, provided the sink has the right drain configuration or can be adapted, and adequate electrical access is available.

How long does a disposal typically last?

With normal use, most units last 8 to 12 years before the motor wears out.

Is it normal for a disposal to run hot after heavy use?

A brief warm-up during extended use is normal, but a unit that shuts off from overheating repeatedly may be undersized or nearing the end of its life.

Can a disposal be repaired if it was installed incorrectly?

In many cases yes, though a poorly supported or improperly connected unit sometimes needs to be reinstalled correctly rather than just repaired in place.
