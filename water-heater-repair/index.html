The Pflugerville Factor

The Local Factor Behind This Problem

Hard water from the area's utility supply is the single biggest reason water heater components fail early here. Scale buildup on a gas unit's burner assembly or an electric unit's heating element is a frequent, preventable cause of no-hot-water calls, and it's the kind of failure a flush and inspection can often catch before it becomes a full outage.

Do You Need This?

Do You Need Water Heater Repair?

No Hot Water At All

Often a failed heating element (electric) or a pilot/igniter problem (gas), not necessarily the whole unit.

Inconsistent Temperature

Water that runs hot then cold mid-shower usually points to a failing thermostat or sediment interference.

Leaking From the Tank or Fittings

A leak from a fitting or valve is often repairable; a leak from the tank body itself usually isn't.

Discolored or Smelly Hot Water

A rotten-egg smell in hot water only is typically a reaction inside the tank's anode rod, not a water-supply issue.

Our Approach

How We Handle It

We start by isolating whether the problem is electrical, gas supply, or mechanical, which for a gas unit means checking the thermocouple, gas valve, and burner assembly. For an electric unit, that means testing each heating element and thermostat individually rather than replacing both at once.

If sediment is the culprit, a full tank flush restores much of the lost efficiency; if a component has actually failed, we replace that part specifically rather than the whole unit, provided the tank itself is still sound.

Your Choices

What Your Options Look Like

Whether a repair makes sense depends heavily on the unit's age and which component actually failed.

Repair

  • Makes sense for units under 6-8 years old.
  • Targeted fix for one failed component.
  • Lower upfront cost.
  • Buys time before a planned replacement.

Replace

  • Makes more sense for units over 10 years old.
  • Avoids repeated repair calls on an aging tank.
  • Better fit if the tank itself shows corrosion.
  • See our water heater replacement page.

Answers

Questions We Hear Often

Is it worth repairing a water heater over 10 years old?

Usually not, unless the fix is inexpensive and the tank itself shows no corrosion; we'll tell you honestly rather than upsell a repair on a unit near the end of its life.

Do you offer 24/7 emergency repair?

Yes, no hot water and active leaks are handled on our emergency line around the clock.

Can a water heater leak cause water damage?

Yes, especially a tank seam failure; if that's already happened, see our water damage restoration page.

Why does my hot water smell like rotten eggs?

That's typically a reaction between the tank's anode rod and certain bacteria in the water, not a citywide water-quality issue.
